  Holm correction.
- **G5b fails.** Mean latency and throughput do not respond either.

## Why the tail did not move (descriptive, post hoc; not a gate)

From `a7/slow_jobs_descriptive.log`:

- **Few slow jobs in any ordering.** Jobs started below 75 °C take a median of 196.67 ms. Per sequence of
  600 jobs, the median number slower than 205 ms is 2.5 (hot_first), 2.5 (cool_first), 2.0 (alternating) and
  2.5 (random).
- **P99 sits on ordinary jobs.** P99 is the 6th largest of 600, so it lands on ordinary 200–202 ms jobs. There
  are 15–18 jobs per sequence above 200 ms in every ordering. P99 does not land on the few throttled ones.
- **Throttling here is shallow.** The 474 HOT jobs followed by a live throttle flag had a median of 202.0 ms.
  In Experiment 2's plateau, jobs ran at 208–212 ms after minutes of continuous load.
- **A few sequences were much hotter.** Some hot_first and cool_first sequences spent up to 35–37 s above the
  knee, with up to 13 and 43 jobs over 205 ms. These are spread across repetitions, so the per-ordering
  medians are unaffected.

These observations explain the result. They do not change it: the confirmatory test is G5 as pre-registered,
and it failed.

## Consequence

Under the protocol's pre-registered gates the pilot ends at **LIKELY KILL**:

- The thermal effect of ordering exists (G4).
- On this hardware and workload it is immaterial to tail latency, mean latency and throughput (G5, G5b).
- The protocol (Phase 10) treats this as a likely kill of the TDAS claim, not as grounds to tune the design
  until something moves.
- Any further experiment, for example longer sequences or hot blocks that reach deeper throttling, would be a
  new, separately pre-registered study. It would not be a rescue of this one.
